Source 1

Title: The influence of AI on consumer behavior: Shaping choices and preferences in the digital marketplace

Publisher: ScienceDirect

Summary: This article examines how consumers respond to AI adoption in services, including their comfort levels, expectations, and perceived benefits. While not directly about job loss, it is valuable because consumer acceptance of AI often drives employer adoption, indirectly contributing to displacement. Readers studying consumer market behavior, service automation, or human interaction with AI will find this source useful. One strength of the article is its combination of qualitative and quantitative data. However, a limitation is its narrow focus on service interactions rather than broader economic impacts, which can be attributed to the fact that consumers are a passive stakeholder in this issue, while the other four groups are active stakeholders. Still, this article helps illustrate how consumer preferences can accelerate automation trends that ultimately reshape the job market.

Source 2

Title: What consumers do and don't want from AI

Publisher: World Economic Forum

Summary: This article explores how consumers are interacting with AI-enabled products and services, emphasizing their values of convenience, affordability, and ethical considerations. It discusses the tension between what AI could offer consumers, like lower costs and faster services, as well as what it should offer in terms of responsible usage. The source's value lies in the credibility of the World Economic Forum, and its balanced, globally oriented analysis of the benefits and risks of AI to consumers, making it ideal for understanding why consumers tend to support AI adoption despite job displacement concerns. Employers and investors studying consumer trends benefit most from giving this article a read. A limitation of the source is its broad global framing, which may not deeply analyze economic trade-offs between markets.
